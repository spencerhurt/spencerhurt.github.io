_Despite past attempts to channelize the river, historic cattle grazing, water diversions, and severe drought, the Gila River has a remarkably intact floodplain near the communities of Gila and Cliff in southwestern New Mexico. The active floodplain facilitates a mosaic of diverse habitat types while sustaining native riparian vegetation; unlike most riverscapes in the southwestern United States, tamarisk and other non-native trees and shrubs have yet to establish along this section of the Gila. The orthophoto above was constructed using drone imagery while studying how the plant community responds to flooding and alluvial groundwater levels at The Nature Conservancy's Gila River Preserve._
